This update adds explicit guidance for spotting when an Octelium Deployment has fewer available replicas than requested, a gap that could leave the system healthy in Argo while silently missing capacity. The new text points operators to the `KubeDeploymentReplicasMismatch` Prometheus alert, shows how to filter by namespace and Deployment, and reminds them that Alertmanager routes the alert to `homelab-notifications` for quick triage.

## Introduce replica deficit detection

The runbook now includes a dedicated paragraph that tells you how to check for a replica mismatch using the `KubeDeploymentReplicasMismatch` alert in Prometheus. If the alert fires while Argo still reports Healthy, you can filter by namespace and Deployment name, inspect the firing state in Alertmanager, and understand why available replicas fall short of the requested count.

## Clarify Service inventory and loss scenarios

The Service remains the canonical inventory for each generated proxy, but the runbook makes explicit that detecting the simultaneous loss of a Service and its Deployment requires a separate desired‑Service inventory. An entirely absent control‑plane or dataplane role also needs an explicit role inventory, so operators are reminded to probe endpoint reachability, public gRPC, and authorization with separate checks.

## Guide verification with the proxy alert check script

Running `bash scripts/ci/octelium-proxy-alert-check.sh` inside the repository's Nix devShell exercises the generated‑proxy failure and recovery fixtures, giving you a concrete way to validate that the alerting and inventory logic work end‑to‑end before a real incident occurs.
